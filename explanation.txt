========================================================
   MISINFORMATION SPREAD ON SOCIAL MEDIA
   SIR Model on Scale-Free Networks — Full Explanation
========================================================

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
1. OVERVIEW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This simulator models how false information (fake news) spreads
through a social network using a modified epidemiological SIR model
combined with a Barabási–Albert (BA) scale-free network structure.

The model captures five distinct node types, two intervention
strategies, and the emergent dynamics of belief propagation under
realistic network topology.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
2. NETWORK STRUCTURE — BARABÁSI–ALBERT (BA) MODEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Real-world social networks (Twitter, Facebook, WhatsApp) follow a
power-law degree distribution — a few "hubs" (influencers) hold
vastly more connections than average users.

The BA model replicates this via preferential attachment:
  → New nodes join the network
  → They are more likely to connect to already well-connected nodes
  → Result: "rich get richer" — hubs emerge naturally

Parameters:
  N  = Total number of nodes (users) in the network
  m  = Number of edges each new node attaches with

Higher m → denser network → faster spread potential


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
3. NODE TYPES (STATES)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

S  — SUSCEPTIBLE (Cyan)
     Ordinary users who have not yet encountered the fake news.
     They can be infected (become Believers) upon contact with
     an infected neighbor.

I  — BELIEVERS / INFECTED (Red)
     Users who have accepted and are actively spreading the
     misinformation. Each time step, they attempt to infect
     susceptible neighbors with probability β.

R  — RECOVERED (Green)
     Users who previously believed the fake news but have since
     stopped spreading it (debunked, lost interest, etc.).
     They are immune to re-infection in this model.

SK — SKEPTICS (Purple)
     Users who are naturally resistant to misinformation.
     They can still be infected but with reduced probability
     (β × skeptic_beta_mult, default 0.3×).
     Represent critical thinkers, media-literate users.

FC — FACT-CHECKERS (Orange)
     Users who actively counter misinformation.
     When a Believer is adjacent to a Fact-Checker, their
     recovery rate γ is multiplied (fc_gamma_mult), causing
     faster debunking. Represent journalists, moderators,
     official accounts.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
4. SIR DYNAMICS — STEP-BY-STEP
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

At each discrete time step T:

  INFECTION PHASE:
  ─────────────────
  For each Believer (I) node:
    For each neighbor:
      If neighbor is S  → infect with probability β
      If neighbor is SK → infect with probability β × 0.3
      (Skeptics are 70% resistant)

  RECOVERY PHASE:
  ────────────────
  For each Believer (I) node:
    If any neighbor is a Fact-Checker (FC):
      recover with probability γ × fc_gamma_mult
    Else:
      recover with probability γ

  FC nodes and Skeptic nodes never change state.
  Recovered (R) nodes cannot be re-infected.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
5. KEY FORMULA — BASIC REPRODUCTION NUMBER R₀
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

              R₀ = (β × ⟨k⟩) / γ

  β    = infection (spread) rate per contact per step
  γ    = recovery rate per step
  ⟨k⟩  = average degree (average connections per node)

Interpretation:
  R₀ > 1 → Each Believer creates more than one new Believer
            → Misinformation GROWS and spreads widely
  R₀ < 1 → Spread naturally dies out over time
  R₀ = 1 → Critical threshold — epidemic boundary

In scale-free networks, hubs dramatically increase ⟨k⟩,
pushing R₀ higher and making containment harder.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
6. INTERVENTION STRATEGIES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

STRATEGY A — FC BOOST:
  Multiplies the Fact-Checker recovery effect by fc_mult (2×–4×).
  Simulates: hiring more moderators, algorithmic debunking,
  official health authority campaigns, rapid response teams.
  Effect: Believers near FC nodes recover much faster.

STRATEGY B — REMOVE HUBS:
  Removes the top hub_pct% highest-degree nodes from the network.
  Simulates: deplatforming influencers, suspending super-spreader
  accounts, banning high-reach pages.
  Effect: Fractures the network, reduces ⟨k⟩, lowers R₀.

Both strategies can be combined for maximum impact.
Dashed lines on the time series chart show intervention results
vs. solid baseline lines.

7. PARAMETERS GUIDE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  N          → Network size (100–400 users)
  m          → BA attachment parameter (2–6 edges)
  β          → Spread rate (0.01 = low, 0.15 = high viral)
  γ          → Recovery rate (0.01 = slow, 0.08 = fast)
  Steps (T)  → Simulation time horizon
  Init I %   → Seed infection fraction (initial Believers)
  Skeptics % → Fraction of population that is skeptic-resistant
  FC %       → Fraction of population acting as fact-checkers
  FC x       → Boost multiplier for fact-checker recovery
  Hubs %     → Top percentile of hubs to remove

8. KEY INSIGHTS FROM THE MODEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

① HUBS ARE THE PRIMARY DRIVER
  Scale-free hubs act as super-spreaders. Removing just 10% of top
  hubs can reduce peak infection by 30–60% in typical runs.

② FACT-CHECKERS NEED PROXIMITY
  FC nodes only help their immediate neighbors. Isolated FC nodes
  have minimal effect — they must be well-connected to matter.

③ SKEPTICS CREATE FRICTION
  A 10–15% skeptic population can meaningfully slow spread even
  without any direct intervention, buying time for recovery.

④ EARLY INTERVENTION IS CRITICAL
  Once I% surpasses ~15–20% of the population, suppression becomes
  very difficult regardless of intervention strength.

⑤ NETWORK DENSITY MATTERS
  Higher m (denser network) drastically speeds up spread.
  Community detection and modular network design can slow it.

9. REAL-WORLD RELEVANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This model is inspired by and applicable to:
  • COVID-19 vaccine misinformation on Twitter/X
  • Political disinformation during elections
  • Health scares (anti-vax, 5G radiation myths)
  • Financial misinformation (crypto pump-and-dump)
  • Crisis situations where rumours cause panic

Policy implications:
  → Platform moderation should target hub nodes
  → Embedding fact-checkers in high-degree communities is efficient
  → Media literacy (creating skeptics) is a long-term defence
  → Early detection of seed Believers is crucial

10. LIMITATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  • Static network: real networks evolve over time
  • No re-infection: some misinformation is cyclical
  • Homogeneous β: real spread rates vary by content type
  • No algorithmic amplification layer modelled
  • Discrete time steps vs. continuous real-world dynamics
  • No cross-platform spread (Twitter ↔ WhatsApp ↔ Facebook)
